## 2. What we measured on our leads [V]

From the 2026-11-17 data (`docs/17` §4 items 1–3 are now built):

- **Fresh investor leads (PTAX-203).** 2,424 investor leads, 1,302 of them from state transfer declarations the Assessor has not loaded; the newest is 11 days old (was 67). Details and the reconciliation are in `docs/15` (PTAX section).
- **Mailing addresses for investor buyers.** The tax roll lists 1,011 more investor leads under the buyer's exact name (owner or tax-bill name); with the 91 where the roll already shows the buyer, 1,102 of 2,424 investor leads have a mailing address (45%, was 4%). 457 of the 1,011 match the purchased parcel's tax-bill name (the bill already goes to the buyer); 554 come from other parcels the buyer holds.
- **Contractor registries.** CDB vendors, IDPH lead and asbestos contractor registries (3,111 rows) add a phone to 34 prospects; 22 are reachable only through a registry. The probe's 23 (`contact-sources-2026-11-18`) was on the previous prospect list.
- **Address routes for company owners.** 131 company-owned leads get the one business Overture lists at the owner's mailing address; the foreclosed-rental registry adds 1 (company agent) and would add 6 more if we shipped private persons' numbers, which we do not.
- **Business pages.** Overture lists a social link for 55% of Chicago-metro places (279,614 of 511,487; 276,243 of those places have a Facebook link, and 94% of all 293,169 links are Facebook). For our matched entities: 84 prospects and 27 company owners.
- **Company websites.** 298 matched sites: 236 readable, 9 blocked by robots.txt, 53 unreachable; 116 readable sites show an email and 194 a phone. A deeper crawl (footer, `/about`, JSON-LD) was not built: the gain is bounded by the 236 readable sites, and robots.txt and rate limits stay.
- **Phone or email on any lead:** 239 of 7,528 (3.2%). Individual owners: 0 by design. For them the letter plus the opt-in reply is the only route.

## 3. Companies (agenda A1–A6)

1. **Illinois Secretary of State.** By statute the SOS must publish LLC and corporation lists as open data at least monthly (805 ILCS 180/50-5(a), P.A. 102-49: "made freely available to the public under an open license, without registration requirement") [V3]. The SOS website says "The use of any process, software, mechanism, tool, device or other means to send automated queries to the website is strictly prohibited" and the entity search is "for individual searches only" [V3 via search snippet; the pages return 403/500 from the cloud VM, recorded and not worked around]. The bulk files carry managers, members, presidents, secretaries, registered agents and addresses, no phone or email [V3, illinoisfiles.org]. **Do:** the founder downloads the LLC and corporation files by hand once a month; a parser joins them to company owners by normalized name (not built: needs one file first). By-hand entity searches for the top leads take about 1–2 minutes each [H].
2. **Company websites.** Already crawled politely (above). The JSON-LD/footer extension is low priority [H].
3. **Email verification.** Spamhaus: verification probes against servers that disabled VRFY "may be considered a hostile act", and verification "does nothing to verify the permission of the recipient" [V3]. CAN-SPAM makes addresses generated "by combining names, letters, or numbers into numerous permutations" an aggravated violation (15 USC 7704(b)(1)(A)) [V3]. **Recommendation:** keep "never pattern-guess" as is; a hosted verifier (NeverBounce $8 per 1,000; ZeroBounce 100 free per month) may check a *published* business email before a manual send [V3 prices; H recommendation]. The rule stays until the founder decides otherwise.
4. **Providers.** Apollo: "for internal business use only. Using Apollo data to power external products, share with customers, or resell is not permitted" [V3]. Snov: no resale or commercial reuse without consent [V3]. Google Maps terms: "will not ... pre-fetch, index, store, reshare, or rehost Google Maps Content" (only `place_id` and coordinates may be cached 30 days) [V3]. Yelp: no storage beyond 24 hours, no building a business database, no free production tier [V3 via search]. BBB, Angi ("or manual process"), Thumbtack, Nextdoor: no copying or marketing use [V3]. Hunter returns "Found" vs "Inferred" emails; only Found would fit our rule, and its terms on passing data to contractors were not checked [V3/H]. **Verdict:** none of these as a stored source; Hunter Found-only: research more if free coverage proves too low (HANDOFF task 4).
5. **Overture.** `socials`, `websites`, `emails`, `phones` are licensed (Meta-sourced places CDLA-Permissive-2.0, attribution required) [V3]. Matching by address added 131 leads; alias/DBA matching from permit records is still untested.
6. **Address-based routes.** Mailing address of other parcels (built, above). Board of Review appeals: dead end [V]. Property managers that hold 10+ parcels (`docs/16`): reachable as businesses through the same Overture/website route; the portfolio test is HANDOFF task 3.

## 4. Social media (agenda B7–B8)

7. **Terms and law.** Meta: "You may not access or collect data from our Products using automated means (without our prior permission) ... regardless of whether such automated access or collection is undertaken while logged-in" [V3, Berkman snapshot of the 2025 terms]. LinkedIn forbids scraping and bot messaging; X forbids crawling without consent (x.com returned 403, read via a court order) [V3]. Van Buren (2021) narrows the CFAA for public pages, hiQ v. LinkedIn ended in a $500,000 consent judgment for LinkedIn, and Meta v. Bright Data (2024) found the old Facebook terms did not bar logged-off scraping before Meta changed them [V3]. **Recommended workflow:** store the business-page URL Overture lists (built, shown as "Business page (message by hand only)"); the founder may open it and send one message by hand from his own account to a *business* page; never collect personal profiles, never automate [H; no clause read today forbids a manual message to a business page].
8. **Social as a conversion channel.** A Brickline (or contractor) business page and Google Business Profile that an owner can check after a letter is allowed and cheap [H]. Meta Custom Audiences require that "you have all necessary rights and permissions and a lawful basis to disclose and use the ... Customer List Data", and Google Customer Match accepts only data "collected in the first-party context" with $50,000+ lifetime spend [V3]. Our owner lists are neither: **do not use**; revisit with an opt-in list of owners who replied [H].

## 5. Individual owners (agenda C9–C10)

9. **Beyond mail.** Skip tracers draw on credit-header and marketing data: BatchData (~$0.02/record at 100k/month, "solely for Customer's own internal business use"), PropStream ($0.10–0.12), REISkip ($0.15); TLOxp: "SUBSCRIBER MAY NOT USE GLBA DATA FOR MARKETING PURPOSES"; IDI bars "marketing purposes" and DPPA data for "direct mail, email, social media, telemarketing" [V3]. TCPA: autodialed or prerecorded telemarketing calls to cell phones need prior express written consent (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(2)); the FCC one-to-one consent rule was vacated (11th Cir., Jan 2025) and removed (Aug 2025); DNC access costs $82 per area code after five free [V3]. Illinois Telephone Solicitations Act: no solicitation calls 9 p.m.–8 a.m.; a live caller must give name, business and purpose and ask for consent (815 ILCS 413/15) [V3]. No Illinois comprehensive privacy law or data-broker registry was enacted as of today (SB0340 was re-referred 5/31/2026) [V3]. **Verdict:** mail plus opt-in reply; no skip tracing, no calls or texts to owners.
10. **Mail effectiveness.** ANA 2024 (via a trade summary): house lists 5–9%, prospect lists 4–5% response; ROI 161% house vs 34% prospect [V3, secondary, self-reported across industries]. For a cold home-repair letter plan on 0.5–2% and measure [H]. 2026 postage: First-Class stamped $0.82, metered $0.78, Marketing Mail 5-digit automation $0.395, EDDM $0.26 (unaddressed, every door) [V3, USPS Notice 123]. NCOA: TrueNCOA $20 per file, Melissa $1.50 per 1,000 ($50 minimum), included with Lob sends [V3]. Handwritten-look envelopes: strong results only in surveys of people with a relationship; RCT evidence for cold sales mail is thin; vendor claims are vendor claims [V3]. Bilingual mail helped only when targeted at households with high Spanish preference and hurt elsewhere [V3, three studies]. **Recommendation:** test, don't assume (`docs/19`).
- **Code-violation owners.** No Chicago ordinance restricting solicitation of owners with a code violation was found; §10-8-510 covers soliciting on the public way, not mail [V3 search; absence is H].

## 6. Compliance spine (agenda D) — rules per channel

Read with `docs/14` §8 (updated with the items marked **new**).

- **Letters (owners).** Contractor-signed; real street address; **new:** a box at the very top, at least 14-point bold, black-outlined, saying it is not a bill, that it is a solicitation, and that the sender is not affiliated with the City or any government agency. 815 ILCS 505/2PP(a)–(b), P.A. 103-87, eff. 2024-01-01, read by us on ilga.gov: "All disclosures and disclaimers appearing on a postcard or letter must be conspicuously located at the top of the postcard or letter, be easily readable in clear and unambiguous language, and be printed in at least 14-point bold-face font in a black-outlined box." [V]. **new:** the contractor's Chicago license number. Municipal Code 4-6-280(2): "the license number shall appear in any advertisement placed by or on behalf of a home repair contractor" [V]. Opt-out line kept; never imitate City letterhead, notice headings or case-number styling (815 ILCS 510/2(a)(3),(5)) [V3]. *Built:* drafts carry the disclosure (shown boxed on the lead page and printed boxed at 14 pt on print-ready letters), the license number from Settings, and a reference code; the mail-merge CSV has a `disclosure_box` column.
- **Email to a business.** Published business address only, sent by hand; says it is an advertisement, real postal address, working opt-out honored within 10 business days (15 USC 7704(a)(4),(5)) [V3]. *Built:* investor drafts now carry "This email is an advertisement from …".
- **Manual call to a business line.** A human dials, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. (our rule; the law allows to 9 p.m.), states name, business and purpose and asks whether to continue (ITSA script, applied to business calls too) [V3/H]. No autodialers, prerecorded or AI voice.
- **Social message by hand.** Business pages only, one message, from the founder's own account, logged in the pipeline; no automation [H].
- **What the vendor agreement must say** [H]: the contractor contacts owners only through the channels above; honors every opt-out Brickline receives within 10 business days; prints the 2PP box and its license number on any letter; gives the "Home Repair: Know Your Consumer Rights" pamphlet and a written contract for work over $1,000 (815 ILCS 513/15, /20) [V3]; never presents itself as acting for the City.

**Where the rules disagree with what the product did (flagged):**

1. *Fixed today:* letters had the not-affiliated line at the bottom and no license number (2PP, 4-6-280).
2. *Open, needs counsel or a test:* our letters offer a "free porch assessment" / "free exterior check". The state's consumer-rights pamphlet warns consumers about "Persons offering to inspect your home for free" [V3]. Keep the written estimate, but test framing without "free inspection" (`docs/19` E2).
3. *Open, needs counsel:* violation letters open with "Public City of Chicago building records show a … repair notice". It is public record and the letter disclaims affiliation, but it is the part most likely to be read as official; have the lawyer read this template first [H].
4. *Open:* "caused to be mailed" in 2PP may reach Brickline itself if it prints or mails for clients [H].
