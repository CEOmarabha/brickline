# Signal Lab — Testing Creative Lead Ideas Against History

*v6, 2026-11-16 (41 hypotheses; round 4 = H29–H33, round 5 = H34–H37, round 6 = H38–H41). Results: `research/findings/signal-lab-2026-11-16.md` (reproducible and deterministic: `uv run python research/signal_lab/lab.py`, ~1 minute; two runs, and a run under a 1 GB DuckDB memory limit, produce byte-identical output; `lab.py H38 H41` writes only `*.partial.*` files). Round 5 source summary and parse check: `research/findings/clerk-zoning-2026-11-16.md`. Round 6 definitions live in `engine/score/events.py`.*

## Why this exists

Any idea for "who is about to need a contractor" can be checked against what actually happened in Chicago over the past 10–20 years before we build anything on it. The lab makes that cheap: one function per idea, a standard set of tests, and a leaderboard. Ideas that fail are just as valuable: they stop us from selling leads that don't work.

## How a test works

| Test type | Question | Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same property | After signal X at a property, how often does job Y happen there within N days? | The same property's own earlier window (removes "busy address" bias) |
| Neighborhood (~160 m blocks) | Does activity next door predict yours? | Blocks with no such activity, same dates |
| Area before/after | After a storm, do nearby blocks pull more permits? | Same blocks a year earlier, and all other blocks over the same windows |
| Cohort | Which kinds of buildings generate the most demand? | All residential parcels |

Verdicts: **Strong** = at least 3× baseline with at least 5 signals per week; **Useful** = at least 1.8×; **Weak** = at least 1.25×; **No signal** below that. Permits only capture permitted work, so every conversion rate is a floor.

**Reading the caveats.** H17 selects complaints by what happened afterwards (a citation within 120 days), so its lift measures the chain, not a forecast made on complaint day. H18 identifies buyers by name string (the sales feed truncates names and investors use one LLC per building), so portfolio operators are undercounted; units are single-parcel deeds. H19 and H20 use the address on the 311 ticket, which is often the parkway or alley, not the building. H21's food-license table has no license type, so ownership changes look like new businesses. All permit rates are floors.

### Product implications

- **Follow-up cadence (adopted).** Citation → permit takes a median 172 days (middle half 97–257, i.e. weeks 14–37), so lead actions now say "follow up at weeks 6, 12 and 24", and owners with an open citation should stay on the list for up to a year.
- **Complaint-lead trade mix.** After a complaint, 17.6% reach a porch/masonry citation and about 6% a porch/masonry permit within a year, so complaint leads should keep being offered to repair trades generally (H07) and not be sold as porch/masonry-specific.
- **Drop storm-proxy and rodent leads.** H19 and H20 do not support any new lead type.
- **Repeat investors.** Show repeat-investor purchases as a relationship opportunity for rehab GCs (an investor tends to reuse its GC), not as higher-probability leads.
- **Seasonal capacity.** Contractors should expect most permitted starts between March and October; pitch winter pilots as "get on the spring schedule".

## Round 3 results (6 hypotheses, 4 new data sources)

New data added to the warehouse: Chicago Food Inspections since 2016 (199,317 inspections, 139,502 at restaurants, with keyword flags on the violation text for pests, plumbing, ventilation/hood and cleaning), CFPB HMDA home-improvement loans for Cook County 2018–2025 (39,104 loans, 15,596 in Chicago tracts), Chicago census tract boundaries (2010 and 2020 vintages), and NOAA SWDI NEXRAD hail detections for the Chicago area 2016–2026 (3,239 cell detections). All sources were reachable; see `docs/data-source-registry.md`.

**Method for the restaurant tests (H23–H26).** A signal is one restaurant inspection (Pass, Pass w/ Conditions or Fail; "Out of Business" for H26) showing the condition, with none at the same key in the previous window. The comparison group is ordinary restaurant inspections without the condition, one per key per window, with the conversion rate **standardized to the signal group's inspection-type mix** (routine canvass, complaint, pre-opening license, re-inspection), because pre-opening license inspections and complaints have very different starting points. Lift is signal conversion divided by that matched rate. Permits and licenses are matched on street address, so another tenant's or the landlord's work counts for both groups.

**Reading the caveats.**

- *H23.* "Conversion" is persistence of need seen at a later inspection, not a purchase. Pest-violating restaurants are inspected more often, which inflates the raw recurrence rate; the headline controls for that by looking only at restaurants that were re-inspected, but the control group there was re-inspected because something was wrong (selection that biases the lift down), so the truth lies between the 1.5× headline and the 3.3× "15+ days" view. Flags are keywords on free text whose item numbering changed over the years; two generic headings (rodent-proof garbage containers, hand-washing supplies) are removed before matching.
- *H24, H25.* Address-level permit matching, no contractor identity, and no way to see unpermitted repairs. The nulls are about permitted work only; a plumber or hood-cleaning vendor could still win these jobs without a permit.
- *H26.* The closure is recorded at an inspection, so some new licensees had already applied (the 19-day median). The license table covers retail food establishments only, so reopenings as a bar, caterer or non-food tenant are missed, and the same-name rule drops genuine ownership transfers under the old name. Both make the conversion a floor.
- *H27.* Area-level and not income-adjusted. HMDA tracts are 2010 through loan year 2021 and 2020 from 2022 (each year is matched to its own boundaries); the 2020 pair overlaps the pandemic; permits attach to parcels only where a PIN or address match exists.
- *H28.* nx3hail is a centroid per tracked hail cell per scan, not a MESH swath polygon: a block counts as under the swath when a centroid of that size was within about 2.5 km that day. Detections come from a box around the city, and days are UTC-6 approximations. Events in the last 125 days of permit data (including the 4-inch storm of 2026-07-27) are excluded so every window is complete.

### Product implications (round 3)

- **Build next: closed-restaurant space list (H26).** The only new winner. It is cheap to compute (inspection feed refreshes daily), the lift is large and stable by year, and the people who want it (build-out GCs, equipment dealers, signage, brokers, restaurant-supply sales) can act in the 12–36 week window in which most later licenses are issued. Report the 31+ day license rate (15%, 5×), not the 32% headline, when quoting a chance of new tenancy.
- **Pest-control list (H23):** offer to a pest-control prospect as a pilot (fresh pest findings, ~45/week, 62% fail) and measure their win rate; do not put a modelled probability in the app yet.
- **Drop:** plumbing and hood inspection leads (H24, H25), HMDA neighborhood index (H27), radar hail leads (H28).

### Why the storm tests failed (and how to retest)

**Round 3 update (H28):** the radar retest has now been run with NEXRAD hail detections (`nx3hail`) instead of spotter points and is also a null (1.04× for hail of 1+ inch within ~2.5 km; 1.12× for 1.75+ inch). The remaining untested piece is gridded MESH swaths (GRIB files, heavy processing) and a non-permit outcome. Original reasoning: NOAA's Storm Events are spotter **points** with coordinates rounded to ~1 km, and hail damage is often repaired through insurance without a permit spike we can see. A fair retest needs **radar-derived hail swaths** (NOAA's Severe Weather Data Inventory "MESH" grids) and, ideally, a target beyond permits (e.g., a roofer client's own lead and job data). Until then, no "storm leads" in the product.

**Method for the business-license tests (H31–H33).** A "site" is an account number plus site number whose first record in the data is an `ISSUE`; 2014–2015 first records are not trusted (pre-2014 sites show only renewals), so signals start 2016. Only premises-based licenses count: peddlers, special events, pop-ups, valet, mobile food, shared-kitchen users, home-based businesses and add-on permits (outdoor patio, late hour, music and dance) are dropped. License groups come from the description and business activity with the precedence auto, food & drink, personal services/beauty, health/fitness/childcare, retail, other. Build-out means the food.py renovation predicate (renovation/alteration, interior build-out text, new construction). Comparison groups are standardized to the signal group's mix (group × year, plus re-let status in H31; class × year in H33), the way the round 3 inspection tests were.

**Method for H29.** Big permit = new construction or renovation/alteration with reported cost of $1M or more (one per address per 540 days; the first one). Class comes from the latest Cook County sale or the residential file, so 2,425 big permits at addresses with no sale since 2010 have no class and no matched control (their follow-on rate is 52.6%). Follow-on trades are permit types where the feed has them (`PERMIT - SIGNS`, `PERMIT - ELEVATOR EQUIPMENT`, `PERMIT - SCAFFOLDING`, `PERMIT - WRECKING/DEMOLITION`, `PERMIT - RENOVATION/ALTERATION`) and text flags on express, easy and renovation permits for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, fire alarm and roofing work (there is no "electric wiring" type in the feed). "Locked in" means a contractor on the follow-on permit has the same normalized name as a contractor on a big permit at that address that day. Scaffolding permits stop appearing in the feed after 2023-11-17, so that row uses a shorter cohort (1,551 signals, 7.2% follow-on, 4% new contractor, 44% of those permits name no contractor).

**Method for H30.** Parcel-quarter rows (32 as-of dates). Features known at the date: years since the last roof permit (<3, 3–7, 7–12, 12+, none on record), class code, building age (<20, 20–50, 50–80, 80+, unknown), a roofing violation in the prior two years, and radar hail detections of 1 inch or more within ~1 km in the prior three years (0, 1, 2+ days, or no location). Rates come from back-off cell tables (full cell, then dropping hail, violation, age, class) with a minimum cell size chosen on a time-split validation (fit through 2017-01, validate 2018–2019; chosen 300 for the main segment and 3,000 for flats) and shrunk toward the base rate with 100 pseudo-observations. Precision is measured per as-of quarter among the top K parcels (ties broken by a hash of parcel and date) and pooled over the 12 test quarters. Test labels are fully observed (the last test as-of date is 2023-10-01).

**Reading the caveats.**

- *H33.* Class-known addresses only (sale since 2010), by street address without unit, so a permit for one tenant counts for the building. "No license in force" uses license terms and cancellations known on the permit date. The control requires no renovation permit within a year either side; the "other permit" control (2.5×) shows how much is just "an active space". The new licensee's type is an outcome, not something the permit predicts. Conversion is a floor: unlicensed tenants, offices and licenses before 2014 history are invisible. The multifamily rows are mostly residential build-outs where a business license is unlikely.
- *H29.* The 14.5× against quiet addresses is mostly "this building is active"; the like-for-like numbers are the own earlier window (3.06×) and the busy-address control (1.5×, but elevator 4.4×, fire alarm 2.5×, roofing 2.0×, signs 1.2×). The "new contractor" shares use exact normalized names cut at 30 characters, so spelling variants count as new (a ceiling on "still open"). Conversion drifted from 65–72% (2017–2019) to 51–54% (2024–2025). Permits are the only record of the work, so electrical, plumbing and HVAC follow-ons are those named in permit text.
- *H30.* The roof-permit bucket is where all the signal is, and it is not a cycle. Main-segment test rates by years since the last roof permit: under 3 years **4.5%**, 3–7 years 1.9%, 7–12 years 1.7%, 12+ years 1.9%, none on record 0.6%. Roofing is repeat work at the same parcels (phased jobs, property managers who permit every year), not an aging roof coming due. Left-censoring: permits start in 2006, so "none on record" means no roof permit in 10 years at the 2016 dates and 17 at the 2023 ones. Building age is known for only 3.8% of main-segment rows (the residential file does not cover commercial; energy benchmarking covers 50k+ sq ft buildings), so it cannot help there; for 2–6 flats it is known for 99% and the model adds nothing over it. Hail is known for 44% of rows (parcels with a pre-2016 geocoded record; using later records would leak the outcome) and adds no precision, in line with H04/H28; the "location flag only" ablation shows its log-loss gain comes from the "no location" bucket, a proxy for pre-2016 activity. Calibration (test period): the middle deciles track within about 20% (deciles 4–8 observe 0.9–1.0× of predicted), the bottom deciles are under-predicted (1.5–1.6× in deciles 1 and 3) and the top decile is over-predicted (predicts 3.9%, observes 2.4%, 0.61×), partly because the base rate fell from 1.08% (training) to 0.78% (test). Sales also feed the universe (a parcel enters once it has a recorded sale), and sales trigger renovations, so rates include newly sold buildings. Roofing violations (0.5% of rows) are too rare to matter. The target is a roof permit, not a roof: commercial re-roofs are often unpermitted or filed with other work.
- *H31.* Survival is a proxy: a renamed tenant or a changed account number counts as closed, a license cancelled after renewal still counts as renewed, and licenses with terms over 25 months before the first renewal (3,548 sites, mostly the 3-year cycle of 2018–2019) are dropped because no renewal can show in months 18–30. Re-let is by street address, so large multi-tenant buildings look re-let; restricting to addresses with fewer than 6 license accounts gives 77.4% vs 61.9% (1.25×). Do not use current Overture presence as a feature: it describes the business after the outcome (survivor bias).
- *H32.* One turnover per address per 365 days, no other licensee in force on the date (storefront-like addresses only). A re-registration under a new account number looks like a turnover; the control (same-account renewals) has fewer build-outs by construction, so "after" lift partly measures a new tenant versus a continuing one. The first-permit "median −77 days" uses the nearest permit within a year either way.

**Method for H34–H37.** Signals use the introduction date (A) or, for passed ordinances, the passage date (B), always from the permit side one address key at a time. An application (App No.) is one project even when an original and a substitute ordinance both exist (earliest introduction, earliest passage); one signal per address per 730 days (the lab's chain rule); only introductions from 2012-01-01 and signals at least 730 days before the newest permit (2026-11-13). **Site:** the address key (first number of a range, like every other source) plus permit addresses on the same side of the same street with a number inside the parsed range. Because the controls count only the exact address key, the like-for-like numerator for "vs comparable addresses" is the exact-address conversion; the site conversion is the headline conversion and the own-earlier baseline uses the same site definition. **Baselines:** (1) the site's earlier 730-day window; (2) comparable addresses: every class-known address (latest sale or residential file; plus an "unknown class" stratum) at semi-annual as-of dates, minus addresses touched by a zoning reclassification within 730 days either side, standardized to the signals' community area × class bucket × year mix (community area from the permit, 311 or license records at the address); (3) the subset of (2) with a non-target permit in the prior year. The headline `lift` is the smaller of (1) and (2) so the verdict does not depend on a baseline that is easy to beat. Passed-ordinance target districts come from the zoning map join above; "no map match" is its own row. H35 GC logic: first $1M+ permit after the signal; "GC new to the address" = none of its contractors with role "general contractor" appears on an earlier permit at the site's addresses (names cut at 30 characters, so spelling variants count as new, a ceiling). H36 targets match `ADU|ADDITIONAL DWELLING|ACCESSORY DWELLING|ACCESSORY UNIT|COACH HOUSE` in permit text (plus any new construction or renovation permit); its baseline is residential addresses (class 2xx, 2–6 flats, 212, 3xx) with no preapproval, same class and half-month. A first independent re-computation of the exact-address conversion from the raw table (no project grouping) gives 10.3% on 6,873 signals vs 10.3% on 6,869 in the lab.

**Reading the caveats.**

- *Recall is low.* Only **9.2%** of the 20,088 new-construction permits issued since 2014 and **11.1%** of the 10,237 $1M+ new construction or renovation permits had a zoning signal at the site in the prior 730 days. The list is a high-precision radar for rezoned sites, not a view of all future work; it does not replace the composite score (E9).
- *Selection and site definition.* Rezoning is requested because someone plans to build, so the lift against ordinary addresses measures "a developer has already acted", which is exactly the value, but it is not a causal effect of the ordinance. The exact-address numbers are the conservative ones; range matching roughly adds two thirds on top. Multi-address ordinances (up to 82 addresses) give one signal per address; the per-application rates are given next to the per-address ones.
- *Decline since 2019.* Conversion fell from 20.6% (2012–2018) to 12.2% (2019–2024); the own-earlier window rose from 1.4% to 2.3% over the same split, so lift vs own earlier narrowed (13–34× in 2013–2018 to 4–9× in 2019–2024) while lift vs comparable stayed 5–12×. Possible causes (not tested): pandemic-era delays, ordinances passed to legalize existing uses or change a use, fewer large assemblies. Use 11–12% (new construction) and 9% ($1M+) as the forward-looking rates, not the 2012–2018 ones.
- *Hindsight splits.* "Later passed" vs "never passed" and the target district (read from today's map) are known only after introduction or passage; the actionable views are all-introduced and passed-from-passage. Matters introduced before 2012 and passed in 2012 are not in the table (collection starts at file year 2012), so 2012 passage counts are slightly low.
- *Permit side.* New construction includes garages and revisions ("revision to permit ..."); addresses are street addresses without unit; a rebuilt lot may be re-addressed, in which case the match is missed (conversion is a floor); permits start 2006, so "ever before" is truncated for early signals. The matter's `sponsor` is "Misc. Transmittal" for 81% of matters (the filing alderman is rarely recorded) and is not used.
- *H35 GCs.* The 99% share of permits with a named GC is a property of big Chicago permits (GCs are named at issue). "New to the address" only compares with permits at the same site; a GC who works nearby is still "new" here.
- *H36.* Only 7.5 months of data, status is as of today (Pre-Certified, Denied and Canceled are hindsight, so all submissions are signals), and ADU text hardly existed in permits before the 2021 pilot, so the own-earlier window is mostly renovation. The lab labels the verdict provisional.
- *H37.* Only addresses whose PIN is found through permits or the tax roll can be compared; a sale after introduction is never used; sale price and deed type are not used beyond the warehouse's arm's-length filter.

**Method for H38.** Series = normalized event key at a place (Park District: park number; CDOT: ward) among held reservations (Approved, Completed, Issued; Tentative, On Hold and Denied are not events). Features for prediction year Y use only years up to Y: held in Y and Y-1 (and Y-2), debut (not seen in any earlier year), organization named; prediction date is 31 December Y. Outcome = any held reservation of the series within ±31 days of the series' first date in Y plus one year; an outcome counts only when its window closed before the newest data (2026-11-16). The headline baseline is the debut cohort's recurrence rate weighted to the held-2-years group mix (so group composition does not drive the lift). Prediction years 2019, 2020 and 2021 are excluded from the pooled numbers (the Park District held 72% fewer events in 2020: 1,527 real rows in 2019, 425 in 2020; 2021 features rest on that gap) and shown in the by-year table. **Method for H39.** Park District lead = event start minus `listed_date`; because the feed was reloaded in bulk on 2026-03-23, the threshold table only counts events starting at least T days after the reload, which has no left-censoring. CDOT lead = event start minus the review-start date, for permits not cancelled or denied whose date has passed (2016+). **Method for H40.** Signal = first renovation/alteration, new-construction or demolition permit-day at an address after 180 quiet days (costliest permit's type and cost), 2017 onward (dumpster data start 2016, so the earlier-window baseline is complete); target = issued, not-cancelled dumpster permit without a street-sweeping note at the same address key starting within 180 days; control = quarterly address-quarters of addresses that have had such a permit at some time with none within ±365 days, standardized to the signals' year mix. GC = first contractor with role "general contractor" on the signal day's costliest permit (names cut at 30 characters); hauler = applicant of the first dumpster permit after the building permit; chance = the market share, in that year, of the haulers the GC used earlier. **Method for H41.** Pre-opening inspection = first `License` inspection of a restaurant license since 2016, joined to the business license with the same license number (ISSUE row); timing uses inspections at least 540 days before the newest record; build-out permit = the latest renovation, new-construction or demolition permit at the address in the 365 days before the inspection.

**Reading the caveats.**

- *Privacy.* Park District requestors and CDOT individual applicants are private people: the requestor column is not downloaded, individual applicants are dropped, event keys and organization names are used for matching only. Aggregates only in the findings. Birthday parties and picnics sit in "Event 1".
- *H38.* The series can recur only if it has a usable name. The Park District's 2020 tier change and the doubling of real-event rows after 2021 mean groups are not comparable across eras (Corporate levels disappear after 2019, Event 1–5 appear in 2020, Promotions and civic venues shrink to a handful); use the by-period table. Historical status is today's: a reservation cancelled before the event is gone, so history covers events that were kept. A recurrence needs the same name and the same park; renamed events and moved venues are misses (conservative). The lift is against debut-year series, i.e. mostly one-off private events; the useful reading is the absolute 53–63%. There is no attendance field: the Event tier is the only size hint.
- *H39.* `:created_at` is when Socrata first saw the row. After the reload it is a first-seen date with up to a week of batch delay, so true creation can be earlier and "visible" leads are upper bounds on what a weekly watcher would have seen. It is not an application date. Rows can be re-created when a reservation is edited, which would reset the date (not tested). The Park District result (events appear at or after the start) is plausible because the feed carries approved reservations; the latest event listed is 2026-12-31, consistent with a calendar-year horizon (not verified), so how a new year loads is unknown until January 2027. CDOT's review start is the earliest application date in the data (submission may be earlier), lead times jump around 2021–22 (possibly a permit-system change; not verified), and cancelled permits are excluded after the fact. Pipeline counts are a single day's snapshot.
- *H40.* The applicant is the hauler: the customer, project and GC are not in the data. Only dumpsters on the public way need the permit; private-property and driveway dumpsters never appear, so conversion is a floor and recall (11–18%) says most dumpster demand has no visible trigger. Street sweeping notes (about 4.5%) are excluded from targets but not from market shares. Permit status is as of today. The 3.2% own-earlier-window baseline is an overlap measure (dumpsters at addresses with earlier work), and the 13.6× control is mostly "this address is a project address"; the lab verdict uses the conservative baseline. Address matching is by street address (no unit), so a dumpster at the curb of the neighbor can be missed or matched to the wrong building.
- *H41.* Pre-opening inspections include new licenses for existing restaurants (ownership changes, no build-out) and cover restaurants only; offices, retail and warehouses have no inspection. The permit has no completion date, so the cleaning window is bracketed, never observed. Weekly volumes carry the posting lag of the newest week. Cleaning is not a licensed activity, so no vendor or contract record exists to test a win rate.

### Product implications (round 6)

- **Event rental and services: do not build a lead product on permits.** Permits do not show new events 3–9 months ahead (H39: the Park District lists a median event 3 days after it started, large Park District events 0 of 750 at 90+ days; CDOT shows a median large event about 6 weeks ahead, about 4 a week at 3–9 months, none beyond 6 months today). The only 3–9 month view is recurrence (H38): about 51 series a year at Event 3 and above (and 7 at the top tier) are predicted 3–9 months out at 59–76% precision, most of them well-known events whose organizers already have vendors, with no organizer contact on the record (0.6% name an organization) and no attendance. "Organizers still pick vendors" cannot be tested with public data. If the founder wants one test anyway, it is a **pilot list for one rental or staging company**, hand-built from CDOT festival/athletic/assembly permits (about 20 large-event applications a week, about two thirds with an organization applicant since 2022; applicant company, event name, dates and street range) plus the recurring-series table, with the company recording who actually buys. Verdict: **reject as a product, pilot only on a buyer's request; needs the archive below to say more.**
- **Dumpsters: reject as a product.** The statistical signal is real (3.0× own history, 13.6× vs project-free addresses), but it reaches 11% of dumpster permits, nine in ten permits never produce one, a third of pairs have the dumpster first, the buyers are 32 haulers (top 5 hold 60%) and GCs stay with their hauler 70% of the time. The one open segment is first-time GCs (38% of linked projects), and permits of $50k+ convert best (12–14%, median 35–62 days): a possible pilot list for a small hauler, not a market. Do not pitch roll-off dumpster leads to the founder's list of contractors.
- **Commercial cleaning: no new product; the H33 list is the cleaning list.** Sell the existing "New business coming" list (median 157 days to the license, about 4.5 months before the opening) and, for restaurants, treat the pre-opening inspection as a last-call trigger for recurring services (28.6 a week; pest, hood and recurring cleaning), not for post-construction cleaning, which has happened by then. Record outcomes on the pilot; no public data can show who the cleaning contractor is.
- **Start a weekly archive of the Park District feed (describe, not implemented; `engine/collect/archive.py` is another agent's).** Add `pk66-w54g` with `:created_at` and `:updated_at` (about 30 MB raw, compresses well) and the CDOT subsets (`DOT_SE` event types; `DOT_DMPSTR` is optional) to the weekly snapshot. It would show whether reservations are edited or re-created (which resets `:created_at`), how status changes (Tentative → Approved, cancellations that vanish today), and, in January 2027, how the new calendar year loads (if the feed then lists 2027 reservations early, the 3–9 month question reopens for Park District events). Then re-run H38/H39 after 6–12 months of snapshots.
- **Data hygiene.** `sync` and `build` include the three new sources (they are in `DATASETS`), about 130 MB of raw pages and about 3 minutes. **`scripts/refresh-data.sh` caches raw pages and clears only the datasets it lists, so `data/raw/park_events`, `data/raw/cdot_dumpsters` and `data/raw/cdot_events` must be added to its `rm -rf` line before the weekly refresh updates them** (not changed in this round). `sync --only park_events` never fetches requestor names.
