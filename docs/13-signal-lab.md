# Signal Lab — Testing Creative Lead Ideas Against History

*v4, 2026-11-16 (33 hypotheses; round 4 = H29–H33). Results: `research/findings/signal-lab-2026-11-16.md` (reproducible and deterministic: `uv run python research/signal_lab/lab.py`, ~35 seconds; two runs, and a run under a 1 GB DuckDB memory limit, produce byte-identical output; `lab.py H29 H33` writes only `*.partial.*` files).*

## Why this exists

Any idea for "who is about to need a contractor" can be checked against what actually happened in Chicago over the past 10–20 years before we build anything on it. The lab makes that cheap: one function per idea, a standard set of tests, and a leaderboard. Ideas that fail are just as valuable: they stop us from selling leads that don't work.

## How a test works

| Test type | Question | Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same property | After signal X at a property, how often does job Y happen there within N days? | The same property's own earlier window (removes "busy address" bias) |
| Neighborhood (~160 m blocks) | Does activity next door predict yours? | Blocks with no such activity, same dates |
| Area before/after | After a storm, do nearby blocks pull more permits? | Same blocks a year earlier, and all other blocks over the same windows |
| Cohort | Which kinds of buildings generate the most demand? | All residential parcels |

Verdicts: **Strong** = at least 3× baseline with at least 5 signals per week; **Useful** = at least 1.8×; **Weak** = at least 1.25×; **No signal** below that. Permits only capture permitted work, so every conversion rate is a floor.

**Reading the caveats.** H17 selects complaints by what happened afterwards (a citation within 120 days), so its lift measures the chain, not a forecast made on complaint day. H18 identifies buyers by name string (the sales feed truncates names and investors use one LLC per building), so portfolio operators are undercounted; units are single-parcel deeds. H19 and H20 use the address on the 311 ticket, which is often the parkway or alley, not the building. H21's food-license table has no license type, so ownership changes look like new businesses. All permit rates are floors.

### Product implications

- **Follow-up cadence (adopted).** Citation → permit takes a median 172 days (middle half 97–257, i.e. weeks 14–37), so lead actions now say "follow up at weeks 6, 12 and 24", and owners with an open citation should stay on the list for up to a year.
- **Complaint-lead trade mix.** After a complaint, 17.6% reach a porch/masonry citation and about 6% a porch/masonry permit within a year, so complaint leads should keep being offered to repair trades generally (H07) and not be sold as porch/masonry-specific.
- **Drop storm-proxy and rodent leads.** H19 and H20 do not support any new lead type.
- **Repeat investors.** Show repeat-investor purchases as a relationship opportunity for rehab GCs (an investor tends to reuse its GC), not as higher-probability leads.
- **Seasonal capacity.** Contractors should expect most permitted starts between March and October; pitch winter pilots as "get on the spring schedule".

## Round 3 results (6 hypotheses, 4 new data sources)

New data added to the warehouse: Chicago Food Inspections since 2016 (199,317 inspections, 139,502 at restaurants, with keyword flags on the violation text for pests, plumbing, ventilation/hood and cleaning), CFPB HMDA home-improvement loans for Cook County 2018–2025 (39,104 loans, 15,596 in Chicago tracts), Chicago census tract boundaries (2010 and 2020 vintages), and NOAA SWDI NEXRAD hail detections for the Chicago area 2016–2026 (3,239 cell detections). All sources were reachable; see `docs/data-source-registry.md`.

**Method for the restaurant tests (H23–H26).** A signal is one restaurant inspection (Pass, Pass w/ Conditions or Fail; "Out of Business" for H26) showing the condition, with none at the same key in the previous window. The comparison group is ordinary restaurant inspections without the condition, one per key per window, with the conversion rate **standardized to the signal group's inspection-type mix** (routine canvass, complaint, pre-opening license, re-inspection), because pre-opening license inspections and complaints have very different starting points. Lift is signal conversion divided by that matched rate. Permits and licenses are matched on street address, so another tenant's or the landlord's work counts for both groups.

**Reading the caveats.**

- *H23.* "Conversion" is persistence of need seen at a later inspection, not a purchase. Pest-violating restaurants are inspected more often, which inflates the raw recurrence rate; the headline controls for that by looking only at restaurants that were re-inspected, but the control group there was re-inspected because something was wrong (selection that biases the lift down), so the truth lies between the 1.5× headline and the 3.3× "15+ days" view. Flags are keywords on free text whose item numbering changed over the years; two generic headings (rodent-proof garbage containers, hand-washing supplies) are removed before matching.
- *H24, H25.* Address-level permit matching, no contractor identity, and no way to see unpermitted repairs. The nulls are about permitted work only; a plumber or hood-cleaning vendor could still win these jobs without a permit.
- *H26.* The closure is recorded at an inspection, so some new licensees had already applied (the 19-day median). The license table covers retail food establishments only, so reopenings as a bar, caterer or non-food tenant are missed, and the same-name rule drops genuine ownership transfers under the old name. Both make the conversion a floor.
- *H27.* Area-level and not income-adjusted. HMDA tracts are 2010 through loan year 2021 and 2020 from 2022 (each year is matched to its own boundaries); the 2020 pair overlaps the pandemic; permits attach to parcels only where a PIN or address match exists.
- *H28.* nx3hail is a centroid per tracked hail cell per scan, not a MESH swath polygon: a block counts as under the swath when a centroid of that size was within about 2.5 km that day. Detections come from a box around the city, and days are UTC-6 approximations. Events in the last 125 days of permit data (including the 4-inch storm of 2026-07-27) are excluded so every window is complete.

### Product implications (round 3)

- **Build next: closed-restaurant space list (H26).** The only new winner. It is cheap to compute (inspection feed refreshes daily), the lift is large and stable by year, and the people who want it (build-out GCs, equipment dealers, signage, brokers, restaurant-supply sales) can act in the 12–36 week window in which most later licenses are issued. Report the 31+ day license rate (15%, 5×), not the 32% headline, when quoting a chance of new tenancy.
- **Pest-control list (H23):** offer to a pest-control prospect as a pilot (fresh pest findings, ~45/week, 62% fail) and measure their win rate; do not put a modelled probability in the app yet.
- **Drop:** plumbing and hood inspection leads (H24, H25), HMDA neighborhood index (H27), radar hail leads (H28).

### Why the storm tests failed (and how to retest)

**Round 3 update (H28):** the radar retest has now been run with NEXRAD hail detections (`nx3hail`) instead of spotter points and is also a null (1.04× for hail of 1+ inch within ~2.5 km; 1.12× for 1.75+ inch). The remaining untested piece is gridded MESH swaths (GRIB files, heavy processing) and a non-permit outcome. Original reasoning: NOAA's Storm Events are spotter **points** with coordinates rounded to ~1 km, and hail damage is often repaired through insurance without a permit spike we can see. A fair retest needs **radar-derived hail swaths** (NOAA's Severe Weather Data Inventory "MESH" grids) and, ideally, a target beyond permits (e.g., a roofer client's own lead and job data). Until then, no "storm leads" in the product.

**Method for the business-license tests (H31–H33).** A "site" is an account number plus site number whose first record in the data is an `ISSUE`; 2014–2015 first records are not trusted (pre-2014 sites show only renewals), so signals start 2016. Only premises-based licenses count: peddlers, special events, pop-ups, valet, mobile food, shared-kitchen users, home-based businesses and add-on permits (outdoor patio, late hour, music and dance) are dropped. License groups come from the description and business activity with the precedence auto, food & drink, personal services/beauty, health/fitness/childcare, retail, other. Build-out means the food.py renovation predicate (renovation/alteration, interior build-out text, new construction). Comparison groups are standardized to the signal group's mix (group × year, plus re-let status in H31; class × year in H33), the way the round 3 inspection tests were.

**Method for H29.** Big permit = new construction or renovation/alteration with reported cost of $1M or more (one per address per 540 days; the first one). Class comes from the latest Cook County sale or the residential file, so 2,425 big permits at addresses with no sale since 2010 have no class and no matched control (their follow-on rate is 52.6%). Follow-on trades are permit types where the feed has them (`PERMIT - SIGNS`, `PERMIT - ELEVATOR EQUIPMENT`, `PERMIT - SCAFFOLDING`, `PERMIT - WRECKING/DEMOLITION`, `PERMIT - RENOVATION/ALTERATION`) and text flags on express, easy and renovation permits for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, fire alarm and roofing work (there is no "electric wiring" type in the feed). "Locked in" means a contractor on the follow-on permit has the same normalized name as a contractor on a big permit at that address that day. Scaffolding permits stop appearing in the feed after 2023-11-17, so that row uses a shorter cohort (1,551 signals, 7.2% follow-on, 4% new contractor, 44% of those permits name no contractor).

**Method for H30.** Parcel-quarter rows (32 as-of dates). Features known at the date: years since the last roof permit (<3, 3–7, 7–12, 12+, none on record), class code, building age (<20, 20–50, 50–80, 80+, unknown), a roofing violation in the prior two years, and radar hail detections of 1 inch or more within ~1 km in the prior three years (0, 1, 2+ days, or no location). Rates come from back-off cell tables (full cell, then dropping hail, violation, age, class) with a minimum cell size chosen on a time-split validation (fit through 2017-01, validate 2018–2019; chosen 300 for the main segment and 3,000 for flats) and shrunk toward the base rate with 100 pseudo-observations. Precision is measured per as-of quarter among the top K parcels (ties broken by a hash of parcel and date) and pooled over the 12 test quarters. Test labels are fully observed (the last test as-of date is 2023-10-01).

**Reading the caveats.**

- *H33.* Class-known addresses only (sale since 2010), by street address without unit, so a permit for one tenant counts for the building. "No license in force" uses license terms and cancellations known on the permit date. The control requires no renovation permit within a year either side; the "other permit" control (2.5×) shows how much is just "an active space". The new licensee's type is an outcome, not something the permit predicts. Conversion is a floor: unlicensed tenants, offices and licenses before 2014 history are invisible. The multifamily rows are mostly residential build-outs where a business license is unlikely.
- *H29.* The 14.5× against quiet addresses is mostly "this building is active"; the like-for-like numbers are the own earlier window (3.06×) and the busy-address control (1.5×, but elevator 4.4×, fire alarm 2.5×, roofing 2.0×, signs 1.2×). The "new contractor" shares use exact normalized names cut at 30 characters, so spelling variants count as new (a ceiling on "still open"). Conversion drifted from 65–72% (2017–2019) to 51–54% (2024–2025). Permits are the only record of the work, so electrical, plumbing and HVAC follow-ons are those named in permit text.
- *H30.* The roof-permit bucket is where all the signal is, and it is not a cycle. Main-segment test rates by years since the last roof permit: under 3 years **4.5%**, 3–7 years 1.9%, 7–12 years 1.7%, 12+ years 1.9%, none on record 0.6%. Roofing is repeat work at the same parcels (phased jobs, property managers who permit every year), not an aging roof coming due. Left-censoring: permits start in 2006, so "none on record" means no roof permit in 10 years at the 2016 dates and 17 at the 2023 ones. Building age is known for only 3.8% of main-segment rows (the residential file does not cover commercial; energy benchmarking covers 50k+ sq ft buildings), so it cannot help there; for 2–6 flats it is known for 99% and the model adds nothing over it. Hail is known for 44% of rows (parcels with a pre-2016 geocoded record; using later records would leak the outcome) and adds no precision, in line with H04/H28; the "location flag only" ablation shows its log-loss gain comes from the "no location" bucket, a proxy for pre-2016 activity. Calibration (test period): the middle deciles track within about 20% (deciles 4–8 observe 0.9–1.0× of predicted), the bottom deciles are under-predicted (1.5–1.6× in deciles 1 and 3) and the top decile is over-predicted (predicts 3.9%, observes 2.4%, 0.61×), partly because the base rate fell from 1.08% (training) to 0.78% (test). Sales also feed the universe (a parcel enters once it has a recorded sale), and sales trigger renovations, so rates include newly sold buildings. Roofing violations (0.5% of rows) are too rare to matter. The target is a roof permit, not a roof: commercial re-roofs are often unpermitted or filed with other work.
- *H31.* Survival is a proxy: a renamed tenant or a changed account number counts as closed, a license cancelled after renewal still counts as renewed, and licenses with terms over 25 months before the first renewal (3,548 sites, mostly the 3-year cycle of 2018–2019) are dropped because no renewal can show in months 18–30. Re-let is by street address, so large multi-tenant buildings look re-let; restricting to addresses with fewer than 6 license accounts gives 77.4% vs 61.9% (1.25×). Do not use current Overture presence as a feature: it describes the business after the outcome (survivor bias).
- *H32.* One turnover per address per 365 days, no other licensee in force on the date (storefront-like addresses only). A re-registration under a new account number looks like a turnover; the control (same-account renewals) has fewer build-outs by construction, so "after" lift partly measures a new tenant versus a continuing one. The first-permit "median −77 days" uses the nearest permit within a year either way.

### Product implications (round 4)

- **Pilot list: "new business coming" (H33).** Weekly list of build-out permits at class-known commercial, mixed-use and 7+ unit addresses with no license in force: address, permit date, cost, work description, GC and applicant names from the permit, class, "typical wait to license: median 157 days, middle half 92–238", "about 1 in 7 gets a new license within a year (21% for commercial/industrial)". It is a targeting list for sign, POS, insurance, cleaning, security and furniture sellers, brokers and subs, not a modelled chance of work, and it overlaps the closed-restaurant list (H26) for food, so de-duplicate. Needs a pilot buyer before building the app screen.
- **Block, not a lead kind: open trades after a big permit (H29).** For the commercial-subcontractor track, a table on `/commercial` ("after a $1M+ permit, 36% see a new elevator contractor, 35% a new electrician, 25% a fire-alarm contractor, 19% a sign shop, median 4–8 months later") plus a weekly list of big permits (~9/week) for elevator, electrical, fire-alarm and sign sellers. Do not offer plumbing, HVAC or roofing on it (about half of those follow-ons are already locked in).
- **Do not productize:** the reroof model (H30), the legitimacy tag (H31, nice-to-have at most) or turnover → build-out (H32).
- **Follow-ups worth testing:** predict the new licensee's group from the permit text for H33; Assessor commercial valuation data (year built, square feet) to retest H30 where building age is missing; a pilot with a sign shop on the H29/H33 lists to measure real win rates.

## How winners reach the product

1. Add the signal to `engine/leads/snapshot.py` as a lead type with its historical rate and timing.
2. Add it to the backtests (`research/backtests`) so it's re-validated whenever data refreshes.
3. Show it in the client app with its evidence and the "why" sentence.
4. Measure real close rates in Results; drop it if pilots don't convert.
